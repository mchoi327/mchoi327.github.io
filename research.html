Research

I study heat transport and thermal management in heterogeneously integrated electronics, combining electrothermal simulation, machine learning, and thin-film thermal characterization.

Electrothermal co-design

Bringing power conversion closer to a processor shortens electrical paths, but also concentrates heat beneath the chip. Power delivery and cooling therefore need to be evaluated together.

I develop automated, self-consistent electrothermal models of distributed vertical power delivery architectures with substrate-embedded microfluidic cooling. These models connect electrical losses, temperature-dependent device behavior, and cooling requirements to evaluate design choices.

In our study of a 48-to-1 V architecture supplying 1 kW, accounting for electrothermal coupling increased the predicted system losses by about 15.8% relative to the initial uncoupled estimate, demonstrating why thermal feedback matters to system design.

Related work: Self-consistent electrothermal modeling · Vertical power delivery design framework · Embedded microchannel cooling

Machine-learning-accelerated thermal modeling

Self-heating and thermal crosstalk affect device performance, yet repeatedly resolving transient temperature fields is computationally expensive.

I develop deep operator network (DeepONet) surrogate models for FinFET arrays and vertical power delivery modules. These models learn mappings between power inputs and temperature fields to support rapid thermal evaluation.

For a 3.5 ns transient response in FinFET arrays, our trained surrogate achieved approximately 2,500-fold speedup over the finite-volume solver, with over 92% of nodal errors below 3 K. Related device-to-circuit work examines how self-heating in gate-all-around nanosheet transistors with backside power delivery affects circuit performance.

Related work: FinFET temperature-field prediction · DeepONet for vertical power delivery · Nanosheet device-to-circuit analysis

Thin-film thermal characterization

Thermal models depend on material properties that can differ substantially from bulk values in nanoscale films. Measuring these properties helps connect material development with device and package design.

I am developing a physics-informed neural network surrogate for frequency-domain thermoreflectance (FDTR) to estimate depth-dependent thermal properties of thin films. My collaborative work also investigates aluminum nitride and vertically aligned hexagonal boron nitride films for thermal management.

Related work: Thermally conductive AlN films · Quasi-ballistic transport in h-BN

Earlier work: turbine rim sealing

At Seoul National University, I constructed and validated a turbine test rig to study hot-gas ingestion and rim sealing. I developed a patented rotor-disk attachment, the “swirler,” to investigate how enhanced wheel-space swirl improves sealing performance.

Related work: Swirl enhancement and rim seal performance
